# Retroscore — Internal Marketing Source Brief

**Source:** Owner-supplied `FinalRetroscorepaper.pdf`, 7 pages. PDF metadata records creation on 12 February 2026.  
**Prepared:** 7 November 2026.  
**Use:** Internal marketing context for Kira. This is a faithful working extraction of the supplied report, not independent due diligence or verification.

## 1. Business identity and model described by the source

Retroscore is described as a Christchurch, New Zealand online retail business focused on retro collectibles and nostalgia merchandise.

The report describes the product mix as:
- Retro electronics and gaming consoles
- Trading cards
- Pop-culture collectibles
- Analogue media
- Nostalgia merchandise

The operating model described is to source inventory with margin headroom, clean/refurbish and quality-check items, track stock through barcode-linked spreadsheets, and sell through online marketplaces.

Sales channels named in the report:
- Trade Me
- Facebook Marketplace
- eBay, described as expansion-ready
- Instagram and other social platforms
- Planned website integration

Operational systems mentioned include structured inventory tracking, dynamic repricing, supplier relationships, and a Fizzly business hub integration. The report states supplier continuity for at least three years, but does not include contracts or evidence.

## 2. Useful positioning themes

These are strategic interpretations of the supplied material, not independently verified market facts.

### Consumer-facing retail
- Curated retro and nostalgia finds, rather than undifferentiated second-hand stock.
- Condition checking, cleaning and refurbishment as potential trust signals, provided current processes support the claim.
- Physical ownership and collectability as emotional territory, without making unsupported claims about market growth or future appreciation.
- Clear, item-specific listings with condition, compatibility, completeness and tested status stated accurately.

### Business-sale / acquisition-facing
- A niche online retail operation with an inventory base and documented operating processes.
- A multi-channel marketplace footprint.
- Inventory systems and supplier continuity as potential transition assets, subject to current verification.
- Growth pathways such as international marketplace sales and workflow automation, presented as opportunities rather than completed capabilities unless evidence confirms delivery.

**Important:** Consumer retail marketing and marketing the business for sale are two different campaigns with different audiences, proof requirements and calls to action. Do not blend them.

## 4. Claims requiring evidence or owner confirmation

Before Kira uses any of the following in public-facing copy, obtain current evidence or explicit owner confirmation:

- Current inventory quantity, retail value, acquisition cost, stock condition and availability.
- Whether every listed item is tested, cleaned, refurbished or quality-checked, and what those terms mean operationally.
- Current status and ownership of Trade Me, Facebook, eBay, Instagram, Google Business and any website.
- Whether eBay international selling is active or merely possible.
- Whether the Fizzly integration, dynamic repricing, inventory synchronisation, performance analytics and automated relisting are live. The source describes automation milestones targeted for May 2026; that date has passed.
- Supplier agreements, duration, transferability and current continuity.
- Customer base size, repeat-purchase rate, community engagement and brand recognition.
- The report's assertion that EB Games generated approximately $52.7m NZD across 38 New Zealand stores. The PDF supplies no citation.
- Assertions that nostalgia demand is rising, Millennials/Gen Z demand is increasing, or overseas buyers pay premiums. These need current market research before being presented as facts.

## 5. Financial consistency warnings

The report contains figures that should be reconciled before any buyer-facing information memorandum or financial advertisement is produced:

1. The executive summary gives embedded inventory gross profit of $18,889.52 and margin of 61%, but no inventory schedule is attached.
2. The three-year projection table reports Year 1 net profit of $12,531, while the DCF table reports Year 1 net cash flow of $9,022. These may use different definitions, but the report does not explain the reconciliation.
3. The report recommends a $45,000–$50,000 asking range and a $38,000–$50,000 strategic buyer range, but separately calculates a DCF indicative enterprise value of $107,658. The assumptions and bridge between these valuations are not supplied.
4. The DCF table's terminal value calculation and underlying five-year cash-flow assumptions are not accompanied by supporting workings.

Do not choose the most attractive figure or silently reconcile these differences. Ask for updated source schedules before using them.

## 6. Kira's operating rules for this business context

- Treat this PDF as an internal, dated source, not a live inventory feed or verified financial record.
- Prefer item-level facts supplied in the current inventory system over aggregate figures in this report.
- Do not invent item availability, condition, prices, provenance, rarity, appreciation, customer demand or sales performance.
- Do not describe planned automation or channel expansion as already operational.
- For consumer campaigns, lead with the actual collectible and its verified condition, value and availability. Avoid business-sale financial information.
- For a business-sale campaign, use a separate buyer-focused brief and require current financial records, asset schedules, platform ownership/transfer details and a confirmed asking price.
- If current research is unavailable, do not describe market conditions as current.
- Where evidence is missing, mark the claim for owner confirmation instead of filling the gap.
